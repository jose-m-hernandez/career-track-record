# A structure read

Before you edit a line, read the whole draft for order. A line edit can improve a sentence in a piece that is in the wrong order, and then you have a better sentence in the wrong place. This guide is the text for that first human read. It is a way of working. No command reads a draft for structure.

## Five questions

1. **What does this piece change for its reader?** Say it in a sentence. A cover letter might make a hiring manager see a records clerk as ready to run a retention schedule. If you can't say it, the piece has no centre yet.
2. **Is the first line specific?** "I am writing to apply" says nothing the subject line didn't. "Last year I moved 12,000 permit files into a system the county still uses" says something only you can say.
3. **Are the beats in a sensible order?** List what each paragraph or section does, in a few words each. Check that one leads to the next, and that nothing is said twice.
4. **Is the strongest true fact where it lands?** The strongest fact belongs where the reader will weigh it, which is usually near the top and again at the close. A strong result buried in the fifth bullet is a placement problem.
5. **Where does it have a gap?** A gap is a place where the reader will ask a question the piece doesn't answer.

## Propose moves, not rewrites

Write down each change as a move: "put the archive project before the counter job", "cut the second paragraph, it repeats the first". Don't rewrite sentences at this stage. A rewrite fixes wording, and the wording belongs to the line edit.

When you find a gap, flag it. Don't fill it with a fact you haven't got. If the reader needs to know the size of the archive and your record doesn't say, the answer is to look it up, or to leave the question open, and not to write a plausible number. This follows rule 5 of the method: a claim is linked to the facts behind it, and one with no link is flagged, not trusted ([METHOD.md](../../METHOD.md)). Check any line you move or add against your claims list ([claims](claims.md)).

## A short piece needs no arc

A two-line message, a one-paragraph profile summary or a short reply needs the first and fourth questions and little else. Don't force a beginning, middle and end on three sentences.

## Order of work

Do the structure read before the line edit, the tone check and the claim gate. The structure read can move a sentence into a different place, and then the others would run on text that changes. The tone check and the claim gate are shipped commands ([tone](tone.md); [claims](claims.md)). The structure read has no command.
