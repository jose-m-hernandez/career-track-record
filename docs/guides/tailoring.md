# Tailoring a résumé from the record

Tailoring means writing a version of your résumé for one posting. The risk is that you start by writing for the posting and end by writing what it wants to hear. This guide puts the record first: you choose from what the record can support, and what it can't support stays open. It is a way of working. No command tailors a draft, and the commands that touch the same ground are named where they apply.

## Start from the requirements

Take the posting and list its requirements, each as one line. For each, do one of two things:

1. Point to the approved claim that answers it. An approved claim is a sentence your claims list marks `approved`. How far it stands depends on the facts behind it ([claims](claims.md), "Beyond the gate"; [METHOD.md](../../METHOD.md), rule 5).
2. Mark the requirement open.

An open requirement is a finding. It tells you the posting asks for something your record doesn't show. It is not a prompt to write a line that sounds right. The keyword scan treats gaps the same way: it reports a term the postings ask for and your copy lacks, and leaves the copy as it is ([docs/specs/keyword-scan.md](../specs/keyword-scan.md), lines 165-169; [keywords](keywords.md)). A gap can stay open when no true line exists.

Invented example. A posting for a records clerk asks for three things.

| Requirement | Approved claim | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Experience with a filing system | "Reorganised the county permit archive, 12,000 files." | answered |
| Customer-facing work | "Staffed the records counter, about 30 visitors a day." | answered |
| Familiarity with a records-retention law | none | open |

The third row is open. You can read up on the law, or leave it out, or say so in a cover letter if you can say it truthfully. You don't add a line that implies you know it.

## One strong story for each

Where several claims answer one requirement, choose the one strongest and use it. Don't stack three bullets about the same thing, because the stack makes the page longer and the point weaker. Strongest means the one whose facts a stranger could check most easily ([grades](grades.md)) and whose result is clearest.

## Say what the draft may say, before you write

Before you write a new sentence, write two short lists: what it may say, and what it may not. Draw both only from the approved claims. If the permit archive claim is approved, "reorganised the county permit archive" is on the first list, and "led a team" is on the second unless a claim says you led one.

A tailored draft selects and reorders. It makes no new fact. Choosing which approved claim comes first, which wording suits the posting among the approved wordings, and which claims to leave out are all tailoring. Adding a figure, a tool or a scope that no claim holds is not.

You can check this with a shipped command. Run the claim gate on the finished draft: it fails any sentence that your list hasn't approved, and a changed sentence is a new sentence ([claims](claims.md); [docs/specs/claims.md](../specs/claims.md), lines 122-126 and 180). That check is on the words of the draft. Whether a sentence answers a posting's requirement is yours to judge.
